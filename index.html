LASIK, explained

Vision correction should begin with a complete picture.

LASIK reshapes the cornea with a laser so light can focus more accurately on the retina. It may reduce dependence on glasses or contact lenses for people with common refractive errors, including nearsightedness, farsightedness, and astigmatism.

It is not the right option for everyone. A thorough eye examination, stable prescription, corneal measurements, general health review, and a candid conversation about expectations are essential before any decision.

◌

Candidacy starts with your eyes

Your surgeon should evaluate corneal shape and thickness, pupil size, tear function, prescription stability, and overall eye health.

◎

Goals matter

Talk openly about work, driving, sports, screen use, and what “clear vision” means in your daily life.

◇

Every procedure has tradeoffs

Ask about dry eye, glare, halos, under- or over-correction, enhancement policies, and alternatives to LASIK.

What to expect

Your LASIK journey, step by step.

A typical experience moves from detailed evaluation to preparation, treatment, and follow-up care. Your surgeon’s instructions always come first.

  1. 1

    Consultation & exam

    Review your vision history, prescription, corneal mapping, tear function, and personal goals.

  2. 2

    Personalized plan

    Discuss the recommended approach, realistic outcomes, costs, alternatives, and potential risks.

  3. 3

    Procedure day

    Follow all pre-procedure guidance and arrange transportation home. The clinical team will explain each stage.

  4. 4

    Recovery & follow-up

    Use prescribed drops, protect your eyes, attend checkups, and report unexpected symptoms promptly.

Choosing a Dallas surgeon

Bring better questions to your consultation.

Your comfort and confidence matter. Look beyond price alone and ask for specific, understandable answers about experience, technology, results, and ongoing care.

Talk with the eye center

Consultation checklist

  • Are you board certified, and what is your specialty?
  • How often do you perform the procedure recommended for me?
  • Why is LASIK—or an alternative—the best fit for my eyes?
  • What results are realistic for my prescription and lifestyle?
  • What side effects and complications should I understand?
  • What do follow-up care and possible enhancements include?

Common questions

Start informed. Decide with your doctor.

These answers are general education—not a diagnosis or substitute for an eye examination.

How do I know if I may be a LASIK candidate?+

Candidacy depends on your prescription, age, corneal measurements, eye health, general health, and expectations. Only a comprehensive evaluation can determine whether LASIK or another option is appropriate.

Will I still need glasses after LASIK?+

Some people may still need glasses for specific tasks, and age-related near-vision changes can occur later. Ask your surgeon what outcome is realistic for you.

How should I prepare for a consultation?+

Bring your vision and medical history, a medication list, and your questions. Ask the clinic whether you need to stop wearing contact lenses before measurements are taken.

What should I compare between practices?+

Compare surgeon credentials and relevant experience, the quality of your evaluation, the clarity of risk discussions, technology choices, total cost, and follow-up care.
